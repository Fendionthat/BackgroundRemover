What do these mean?
Erase / Restore
Erase paints transparent; Restore brings pixels back from the original image. Both work in either Brush or Magic Wand mode below.
Brush mode
Paints exactly where you drag, nothing more. Good for freehand touch-ups, or for precise spots where Magic Wand grabs too much (e.g. two same-color areas that turn out to be touching, like a skirt and shirt of the same white).
Magic Wand mode
Click once and it grows through the whole connected area of similar color, stopping at outlines. Great for flat-color art — but if two parts of the subject share a color AND touch with no outline between them, it'll follow that connection further than you want. If that happens, undo and switch to Brush mode, or lower the tolerance.
Brush size
How big an area each click/drag touches. In Magic Wand mode, this is the size of the starting spot — it can still grow much bigger from there.
Softness (Brush mode)
How feathered the brush edge is. 0% is a hard-edged cut/copy; higher values fade out gradually, which blends better with soft/blurry edges like hair or fur.
Magic wand tolerance (Magic Wand mode)
How different two neighboring pixels can be and still count as "the same area." Low tolerance only spreads through near-identical color (safer, stays inside real edges/outlines). High tolerance spreads more freely across shading and gradients, but risks leaking past soft or anti-aliased edges into unrelated same-color areas.
