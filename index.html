What do these mean?
Erase / Restore
Erase paints transparent; Restore brings pixels back from the original image. Both work in either Brush or Magic Wand mode.
Brush mode
Paints exactly where you drag, nothing more. Good for freehand touch-ups, or for precise spots where Magic Wand grabs too much (e.g. two same-color areas that turn out to be touching, like a skirt and shirt of the same white).
Magic Wand mode
Click once and it grows through the whole connected area of similar color, stopping at outlines. Great for flat-color art — but if two parts of the subject share a color AND touch with no outline between them, it'll follow that connection further than you want. If that happens, undo and switch to Brush mode, or lower the tolerance.
Brush size
How big an area each click/drag touches. In Magic Wand mode, this is the size of the starting spot — it can still grow much bigger from there.
Softness (Brush mode)
How feathered the brush edge is. 0% is a hard-edged cut/copy; higher values fade out gradually, which blends better with soft/blurry edges like hair or fur.
Magic wand tolerance (Magic Wand mode)
How different two neighboring pixels can be and still count as "the same area." Low tolerance only spreads through near-identical color (safer, stays inside real edges/outlines). High tolerance spreads more freely across shading and gradients, but risks leaking past soft or anti-aliased edges into unrelated same-color areas.

How does this work?
What it is
An AI model (SlimSAM) that picks out one object at a time by its actual shape, not just its color -- useful where Magic Wand grabs too much or too little because the object doesn't have flat, uniform color. Works on a fresh photo right away, no other step needed first.
Drag a box
Draw a rough box around the object. The AI finds its precise edges inside that box and shows the result as a highlighted preview -- nothing is applied to the image yet.
Refine with points
Click inside the highlighted area (or just outside it) to add a point that should be included; Shift+click to add a point that should be excluded. Each click quickly re-runs just the refinement step, not the whole analysis, so it stays responsive. Dragging a new box starts a fresh selection and clears previous points.
Move
Once a selection is highlighted, drag from inside it (instead of dragging a new box) to pick it up and reposition it -- a dim red outline marks where it's moving from. Nothing is applied to the image until you hit Apply.
Keep only this
Check this box before Apply to erase everything on the canvas except the current selection, instead of just erasing/restoring the selection itself. Combine it with Move to cut an object out and place it precisely.
Apply / Cancel
Apply writes the current selection into the image -- using Erase or Restore above, or "Keep only this" if checked. Cancel discards the selection with no changes made. Either way it's a single undo-able step.
First use
This model is only downloaded the first time you click "Box Select" (a small one-time download, shown in the progress bar below) -- not part of the initial page load.
